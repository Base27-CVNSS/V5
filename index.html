Mathematical foundation

Theorem. Proof. Limitations.

The following claims have different mathematical status. They should not be combined into an unsupported statement that a practical all-convex global V5 grid has already been solved.

Theorem 1 / weak existence

Binary bent-chord lemma

Triangulate a simple pentagon from a shared fan vertex A. With Q on the opposite edge CD and T slightly off chord AQ, the cut A–T–Q creates two strict, generally nonconvex pentagons.

P₅ → 2P₅ → 2⁵P₅ = 32P₅

Repeat five binary subdivisions at each level for any finite number of levels.

Theorem 2 / spherical transfer

12 roots cover the sphere

For a convex dodecahedron D centered at the origin, the radial projection is a homeomorphism from its surface onto a sphere of radius R.

ρ(x) = R · x / ‖x‖

Each straight planar edge maps to a great-circle arc. Coverage and non-overlap of cell interiors are preserved.

Necessary condition

Euler degree budget

For any all-pentagon, edge-to-edge spherical cell decomposition, the vertex degrees cannot all remain three beyond the twelve-cell base level.

Σ (deg(v) − 3) = F/2 − 6

At level r, F = 12·32ʳ, giving an excess-degree budget of 6(32ʳ − 1).

Open research

Strong V5-32

Does there exist a global refinement of convex, edge-to-edge pentagons with uniformly bounded aspect ratio and shrinking diameters across arbitrarily many levels?

max diam(Cᵣ) = O(32−r/2) ?

The current constructive theorem does not establish this.

Scientific integrity: A 32-child weak refinement is possible, but full fivefold rotational symmetry is incompatible with 32 convex children; five unsplit parent edges are incompatible with an edge-to-edge 32-cell partition. These obstructions guide research and do not imply a strong solution exists.

V5 Technical Preprint · Research Draft · October 2026

V5-32: Constructive Weak Pentagonal Refinement on the Sphere and Topological Obstructions

Long Ngo · Vietflexmap · 2026

Abstract

We establish an elementary constructive existence theorem for weak nested 32-ary pentagonal partitions of the sphere. Starting with twelve faces of a convex dodecahedron, a bent-chord subdivision is iterated to obtain 32 nondegenerate pentagonal descendants per parent. Radial projection preserves the nested partition, yielding 12·32ʳ spherical cells at each finite resolution r. The construction permits nonconvex cells and hanging nodes, and does not establish uniform shape regularity, edge-to-edge conformity or an operational DGGS.

1. Main existence result

For every finite r ≥ 0, there exists a weak spherical partition into exactly 12·32ʳ strict spherical pentagons. Each parent is the union of exactly 32 children with pairwise disjoint interiors.

S² = ⋃ Ci    ;    int(Ci) ∩ int(Cj) = ∅

2. What remains unproved

We do not prove that all children can be simultaneously convex, strictly edge-to-edge, shape regular, or asymptotically fine. Efficient spherical point indexing and neighbor calculation remain future work.

3. Reproducibility

Exact-rational planar implementation, automated regression tests, and a bibliography of pre-existing mathematical and DGGS principles are included in the repository.
